Nigeria Disease Surveillance Analysis

Project Overview

This project presents an analysis of simulated disease surveillance data from 10 Nigerian states covering 2024–2025. The project was developed in Power BI to explore disease patterns, demographic characteristics, geographic distribution, temporal trends, and reported case outcomes.

The analysis includes data cleaning and transformation, data modelling, DAX calculations, data visualization, and public-health interpretation.

Note: The dataset used in this project is simulated and was created for portfolio and learning purposes. It does not represent official Nigerian disease surveillance data.

Objectives

* Examine the distribution of reported disease cases across states.
* Identify patterns by age and sex.
* Analyze changes in reported cases across 2024 and 2025.
* Identify the most frequently reported diseases.
* Examine reported case outcomes, including recovery, discharge, treatment, and deaths.
* Generate data-informed public-health recommendations.

Tools & Skills

* Power BI
* Power Query — data cleaning and transformation
* DAX — calculations and measures
* Data Modelling — relationships and analytical structure
* Data Visualization
* Public Health Data Interpretation

Key Insights

* A total of 561 cases were reported across 10 states. Cases were relatively evenly distributed, with Lagos recording the highest number (67; 11.9%) and Plateau the lowest (41; 7.3%).
* Reported cases in 2025 were lower than in 2024 from Q1–Q3, while cases increased by 20% from Q3 (60 cases) to Q4 (72 cases) in 2025.
* Males recorded more reported cases than females, with a 6.9 percentage-point difference. Approximately 5.7% of cases had an unknown sex.
* The 0–10 age group recorded the highest number of reported cases, followed by individuals aged 81 years and above, with 81 cases.
* Cholera recorded the highest number of reported cases among the diseases included in the dataset.
* Kaduna recorded the highest number of deaths, with 77 deaths reported across the two-year period. Approximately 50% were male, 45.5% female, and 4.5% had an unknown sex.
* Across 2024 and 2025, reported outcomes included 281 recovered (50.1%), 89 under treatment (15.9%), 77 deaths (13.7%), 74 discharged (13.2%), and 40 with an unknown outcome (7.1%).

Recommendations

1. Prioritize interventions for vulnerable age groups: Strengthen age-specific surveillance and interventions for children aged 0–10 years and older adults aged 81 years and above. Health education for parents and caregivers should be prioritized, alongside appropriate support and access to healthcare for older adults.
2. Strengthen cholera prevention and control: Given that cholera recorded the highest number of reported cases, prevention and control measures should be strengthened through health education, safe water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) promotion, early detection, and timely case management.
3. Strengthen health-system capacity across states: Since cases were relatively distributed across the 10 states, surveillance and response capacity should be maintained across all states, with attention to healthcare facilities, trained personnel, equipment, and other essential resources.
4. Improve data quality and reporting: The presence of unknown and incomplete records highlights the need for more accurate and complete surveillance reporting. Regular data-quality checks, staff training, and feedback mechanisms should be encouraged to improve the reliability of surveillance data.
5. Investigate the increase in reported cases: The 20% increase from Q3 to Q4 2025 should be further investigated to determine whether it reflects seasonal patterns, changes in disease transmission, improved reporting, or other factors.


Data Limitations

* The dataset is simulated and does not represent official surveillance data.
* Some demographic and outcome fields were recorded as unknown or incomplete.
* The analysis describes patterns in reported cases but does not establish causality.
* Differences in reporting completeness may influence observed patterns across states and demographic groups.
